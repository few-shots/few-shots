What is the shortest Golomb ruler with 29 marks?

## Problem

A Golomb ruler with n marks is a set of n distinct integers (the marks) such that the n(n − 1)/2 differences between pairs of marks are pairwise distinct. Its length is the largest mark minus the smallest mark.

For n = 29 (`[params] order` in `problem.toml`), find a Golomb ruler with 29 marks whose length is as small as possible. There are 29 · 28 / 2 = 406 differences. The score is the length, an integer.

Shifting every mark by the same integer and reflecting (x → c − x) keep the differences, up to sign, and the length. The canonical form below identifies these copies.

The score is an integer, so the next possible score below the baseline is 622.

The least possible length G(29) is not known. The upper bound 623 is the baseline below. Lower bounds:

- 406, since the 406 differences are distinct positive integers no larger than the length.
- 567: [Carter, O'Bryant and Tao, arXiv:2610.36207 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36207) state in the abstract that a Sidon set with k ≥ 1 elements has diameter at least k²(1/(1 + 0.945/√k) − 0.945/√k) − 1. A Golomb ruler is a Sidon set and its diameter is its length; for k = 29 the right side is 566.87…, so the length is at least 567. The proof is not checked here.
- 587, derived here from G(28) = 585. [distributed.net](https://www.distributed.net/News) reports that its exhaustive search OGR-28 was completed in 2022 and proved the optimal 28-mark ruler, and [Rokicki and Dogon](https://www.gathering4gardner.org/g4g12gift/Rokicki_Dogon-Larger_Golomb_Rulers.pdf) (p. 1) give its length as 585. Let x_0 < x_1 < … < x_28 be the marks of a 29-mark Golomb ruler of length L, and a = x_1 − x_0, b = x_28 − x_27. Removing x_0, or x_28, leaves a 28-mark Golomb ruler of length L − a, or L − b, so L − a ≥ 585 and L − b ≥ 585. The differences a and b come from different pairs of marks, so a ≠ b and max(a, b) ≥ 2. Hence L ≥ 587.

The strongest published lower bound for 29 marks is not confirmed here. The bounds play no part in scoring.

## Why it matters

A set of integers whose pairwise differences are distinct is a Sidon set; Erdős and Turán, On a problem of Sidon in additive number theory and on some related problems, J. London Math. Soc. 16 (1941) 212–215 (reference [6] of [Rokicki and Dogon](https://www.gathering4gardner.org/g4g12gift/Rokicki_Dogon-Larger_Golomb_Rulers.pdf)), studied how large such a set can be. For a fixed number of marks, the shortest Golomb ruler is a benchmark in constraint programming ([CSPLib prob006](https://www.csplib.org/Problems/prob006/)), and CSPLib lists sensor placement for X-ray crystallography and radio astronomy among its applications.

Shortest lengths are proven up to 28 marks. distributed.net settled the cases 24 to 28 by exhaustive search, completing OGR-24 in 2004 and OGR-28 in 2022 after eight and a half years ([distributed.net news](https://www.distributed.net/News); [Rokicki and Dogon, §4, p. 6](https://www.gathering4gardner.org/g4g12gift/Rokicki_Dogon-Larger_Golomb_Rulers.pdf)). For more marks, the shortest known rulers come from the finite-geometry constructions of [Singer, A theorem in finite projective geometry and some applications to number theory, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 43 (1938) 377–385](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1938-1501951-4) (projective plane) and of Bose (affine plane). [Rokicki and Dogon, *Larger Golomb Rulers*](https://www.gathering4gardner.org/g4g12gift/Rokicki_Dogon-Larger_Golomb_Rulers.pdf) (written for the G4G12 gift exchange; the PDF is from 2016) report that James B. Shearer implemented these constructions around 1986 and tabulated the best rulers they give up to 160 marks (p. 2), that these constructions give the best known rulers for all sizes above 16 marks (p. 5), and state Hypothesis A: for every n > 16 the optimal Golomb ruler can be generated by an affine or projective plane construction (p. 6).

For 29 marks the shortest ruler these constructions give has length 623, from Singer's construction with q = 29 (see Baseline). A 29-mark Golomb ruler of length 622 or less is shorter than every 29-mark ruler in these sources.

## Certificate

- File name `certificate.json`, shaped like `{"marks": [0, 3, 28, …]}`, with no other keys.
- `marks` is a list of exactly 29 JSON integers, in strictly increasing order, each between 0 and 10^15 inclusive (`[params] max_mark`). The smallest mark need not be 0. Booleans, strings, fractions, exponents (`3.0`, `3e0`), `NaN` and `Infinity` are rejected; `-0` is the integer 0.
- All 406 differences x_j − x_i (i < j) must be distinct.
- The score is x_28 − x_0, the largest mark minus the smallest, as a decimal integer string, for example `"623"`.
- The file is UTF-8 JSON without a byte order mark, at most 65,536 bytes, nested at most 8 levels, with integers of at most 18 ASCII digits and no duplicate keys (keys are compared after JSON unescaping).
- Canonical form. Let x_0 < … < x_28 be the marks and L = x_28 − x_0.
  1. Shift: A = [x_0 − x_0, x_1 − x_0, …, x_28 − x_0], so A starts at 0 and ends at L.
  2. Reflect: B = [L − A_28, L − A_27, …, L − A_0], also increasing, starting at 0 and ending at L.
  3. Keep the smaller of A and B in lexicographic order: compare entry by entry from the first, as integers. (This is Python's ordering of lists of integers.) (A = B is impossible for a Golomb ruler with 3 or more marks, since it would give x_1 − x_0 = x_28 − x_27.)
  4. The canonical object is `{"marks": C}` with C the kept list.
  5. `canonical_hash` is the lowercase hex SHA-256 of that object written as JSON with no whitespace at all, integers in plain decimal, ASCII bytes, no trailing newline; in Python, `json.dumps(obj, sort_keys=True, separators=(",", ":"), ensure_ascii=True).encode("ascii")`.

  So two certificates have the same hash exactly when one ruler is a shift, or a shift of the reflection, of the other. Whitespace and escapes in the key do not enter the hash. A tie with the record is not an improvement either way.

  Worked example (4 marks, only to show the computation): the marks `[10, 14, 17, 18]` give A = `[0, 4, 7, 8]`, L = 8 and B = `[0, 1, 4, 8]`; B is smaller, so the hashed bytes are `{"marks":[0,1,4,8]}`, with SHA-256 `0b23813710787f97546f927f9dcc3f0c5e93035b0e9e1f9d3209da223d3e95ae`. For the baseline, A is the certificate itself and B begins `[0, 7, 11, …]`, so A is kept; the hashed bytes are `{"marks":[0,3,28,33,41,95,156,158,193,207,233,252,267,338,339,355,394,403,421,473,479,502,523,570,580,592,612,616,623]}` (119 bytes), and `canonical_hash` is `e4ad2bc601c9451ea6e8e0bd3af4abcb23bcc5a3e7d2a2c228a33ebc26f0ed9b`.

## Baseline

Length 623, with the marks

0, 3, 28, 33, 41, 95, 156, 158, 193, 207, 233, 252, 267, 338, 339, 355, 394, 403, 421, 473, 479, 502, 523, 570, 580, 592, 612, 616, 623.

The ruler comes from [Singer's construction (1938)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1938-1501951-4) for the projective plane of order q = 29, as described by [Rokicki and Dogon](https://www.gathering4gardner.org/g4g12gift/Rokicki_Dogon-Larger_Golomb_Rulers.pdf) (§2–3). Take a primitive element α of GF(29³). The exponents i modulo q² + q + 1 = 871 for which α^i lies in a fixed 2-dimensional GF(29)-subspace of GF(29³) form a set of q + 1 = 30 residues whose 870 differences modulo 871 are all distinct. Multiply this set by a unit modulo 871, list the 30 residues in increasing order, take 29 cyclically consecutive residues (drop one) and subtract the first modulo 871. Over all units and all windows the shortest result has length 623, and the ruler above, up to reflection, is one of the windows of that length. The 29 in "order 29" is the order of the projective plane; the modular ruler has 30 marks and the 29-mark ruler is cut from it.

Shearer's table of these constructions up to 160 marks ([IBM research site](http://www.research.ibm.com/people/s/shearer/grule.html), cited by Rokicki and Dogon as reference [10]) is offline, and the full data of Rokicki and Dogon ([cube20.org/golomb](http://cube20.org/golomb/)) was not checked, so the 29-mark row of either table is not confirmed here. The two reviews of this problem ([proposal issue #49](https://github.com/few-shots/few-shots/issues/49)) each rebuilt this ruler independently from the construction; one of them scanned all units and windows of the Singer and Bose constructions for q = 29, 31 and 32 and found no 29-mark ruler shorter than 623.
